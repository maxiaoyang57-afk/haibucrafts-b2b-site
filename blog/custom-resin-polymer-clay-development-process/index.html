Custom Resin & Polymer Clay: From Buyer Brief to Approved Production

Start with the product your customers need. Then agree the material route, sample decisions and order records that turn a design idea into a clear wholesale specification.

A useful custom project starts with an application, a purchasing requirement and a decision process. A drawing alone cannot settle material, cost, packing or production suitability. HAIBU reviews resin and polymer clay requests against the intended use, selected design and order scope, then confirms the development steps that apply. The guide below explains what to send, what to clarify and when to approve the next stage.

01

Start with the buyer’s product brief

Explain where the component will be used: a craft kit, decorative accessory, slime assortment or another application. Include the destination market, target audience, required arrival window and estimated quantity with its unit. Pieces, grams, bags and sets are different quotation bases. For an existing design, send the exact SKU and product link; for a new design, send authorized artwork, reference images or a dimensioned sketch. Separate essential features from preferences so alternatives can be discussed without losing the purpose of the project.

Buyer input
Design reference, application, dimensions, quantity, destination and packing idea.
HAIBU review
HAIBU clarifies missing specifications, reference-image limitations and who will approve changes.
Stage output
One project brief with a reference number or design identifier.
Your decision
Confirm the brief and your right to use all supplied artwork, logos and design elements.
02

Agree feasibility and development terms

Before development starts, compare an existing product or available tooling option with a genuinely new design. Changing colors or packing may follow a different route from changing the physical shape. The review should identify features that need adjustment, proposed material, sample scope and the sequence of approvals. Confirm development, drawing, tooling and sample charges where applicable, revision scope, sample shipping and estimated timing in writing. MOQ and bulk pricing must be assessed for the defined design and quantity. Tooling ownership, storage, permitted reuse and any exclusivity are governed only by the agreed project terms.

Buyer input
Approved brief, target quantity and priorities for cost, detail and timing.
HAIBU review
HAIBU reviews practical options and identifies unresolved technical or commercial points.
Stage output
A proposed development route and written scope for the next stage.
Your decision
Approve that scope and any applicable charges before work proceeds.
03

Resin route: define the complete shape

For a custom resin charm, review the front, side and back rather than the front illustration alone. Specify overall dimensions, thickness, raised detail, back profile and any hole or attachment position. A flatback, drilled part and dimensional charm are different requirements. Discuss color placement, surface texture and gloss or matte direction using agreed references. Where a drawing or 3D model is appropriate, approve the relevant views and dimensions before new tooling is made. Existing tooling may avoid that step when the required shape already exists and its use is agreed. New mold development is conditional on design feasibility and the confirmed scope.

Buyer input
Shape views, back or hole requirements, scale, color references and finish direction.
HAIBU review
HAIBU checks design detail, proposed construction and existing versus new tooling needs.
Stage output
An agreed resin specification and drawing or model approval where applicable.
Your decision
Approve the shape revision and any required tooling step before sampling.
04

Polymer clay route: define pattern, form and mix

For polymer clay, first distinguish thin slices, sprinkle-style mixes and three-dimensional pieces. Specify the motif, palette, dimensions and thickness or overall form. For a mix, list required components, excluded designs and target ratios; say whether every individual pack must contain every motif. Small pattern details, color boundaries and slice thickness need review against a physical sample. Not every polymer clay project needs a 3D model or a new mold. Pattern preparation, forming or tooling steps depend on the proposed product. HAIBU confirms the suitable route after reviewing the design, size and quantity instead of applying the resin sequence automatically.

Buyer input
Motif artwork, color direction, slice or 3D format, dimensions, mix ratio and packing unit.
HAIBU review
HAIBU reviews pattern clarity, production approach and assortment feasibility.
Stage output
A polymer clay development brief with the relevant preparation and sample steps.
Your decision
Confirm the design and mix priorities, including any agreed variation.
05

Separate a digital proof from a physical sample

A drawing, render or artwork proof records design intent. It does not establish the feel, actual color, finish, scale or behavior of the finished material. Use digital approval to settle the features that can be reviewed on screen, then move to the agreed physical sample stage. For resin, that may follow approved modeling and tooling. For polymer clay, it follows the confirmed pattern or forming route. Sample finishing and checking should address the agreed dimensions, visible details, color direction and presentation. If a feature differs from the brief, identify it before dispatch rather than letting a sample silently redefine the specification.

Buyer input
The current approved design files and sample requirements.
HAIBU review
HAIBU checks the sample against the development brief and records known differences.
Stage output
An identified sample revision, supporting photos and agreed dispatch details.
Your decision
Confirm which physical samples you need and how you will evaluate them.
06

Evaluate, revise and approve one sample version

After delivery, review the physical sample in your intended application. Check size, thickness, appearance, back or attachment fit and relevant handling requirements. If the component will contact another material, include that actual material in your evaluation. For polymer clay mixtures, inspect both the pieces and the proposed assortment; for resin, include the rear surface and dimensional features. Return consolidated feedback with marked photos and measurable changes. HAIBU reviews the requested revisions and confirms any effect on charges or schedule before another round. Keep superseded versions clearly marked so production is never based on a mixture of approvals.

Buyer input
Application feedback, marked photos and one consolidated revision list.
HAIBU review
HAIBU identifies what can change and whether a revised physical sample is needed.
Stage output
A final sample reference, specification revision and written approval record.
Your decision
Approve that exact version in writing. Sample approval does not place a bulk order.
07

Confirm bulk terms and quality checkpoints separately

Use the approved sample as the reference for a separate bulk quotation and order confirmation. Agree quantity, unit price, MOQ, payment terms, production timing and delivery scope for that order. Define the checks that matter: resin shape, back, hole position and finish; polymer clay pattern, dimensions, thickness and mix balance, as applicable. Record any agreed tolerances, inspection method and handling of differences before production. Ask about documents against the exact SKU, material, project and destination market. Visual checking and sample approval do not establish safety certification or replace applicable testing. Any testing requirement needs its own confirmed scope.

Buyer input
Approved sample record, final quantities and product-specific inspection requirements.
HAIBU review
HAIBU aligns the order specification, production checkpoints and document scope.
Stage output
Written bulk terms and agreed acceptance criteria for the order.
Your decision
Approve the commercial order and quality scope before bulk production begins.
08

Approve the selling unit, labels and cartons

Define whether the product is supplied by piece count or net weight and how those units become bags, jars, sets or cartons. Net weight alone does not guarantee a fixed piece count. For mixed packs, specify the ratio and any permitted variation. Review labels, barcodes, buyer artwork, packing identification and required warnings before printing. Consider protection for raised resin details or thin polymer clay pieces within the agreed transport arrangement. Where packaging affects presentation or use, request a packed sample or other agreed verification. Buyer approval of label content should be based on the destination and application requirements.

Buyer input
Pack count or weight, artwork revision, label content and carton instructions.
HAIBU review
HAIBU checks packout feasibility and reconciles SKU, pack and carton quantities.
Stage output
An approved packing specification tied to the same product revision.
Your decision
Approve artwork and packout details before the relevant packing work starts.
09

Keep delivery and reorders tied to the approved version

Before shipment handover, confirm the final quantities, carton information, agreed checks and required shipping documents. Clarify the delivery method, destination and responsibilities under the order terms. Keep the approved sample identifier, product specification, packing files and accepted changes together. On a reorder, quote that version and list any changes to color, size, motif ratio, packaging, use or market. An earlier approval should not automatically cover a revised design or a different application. HAIBU reviews current feasibility, availability, commercial terms and any affected documentation for the new order.

Buyer input
Order reference, delivery requirements and the approved product and packing records.
HAIBU review
HAIBU confirms shipment information and identifies changes that need a new review.
Stage output
A traceable order handover and a clear reference for future inquiries.
Your decision
Confirm handover details; flag every requested change when planning a reorder.
This is a project-planning guide, not a universal production promise. Materials, development steps, fees, MOQ, timing, tolerances, tooling terms and documents are confirmed for the specific project. Start with your requirements so the appropriate resin or polymer clay route can be reviewed.

Use this in your inquiry.

  • Authorized artwork or exact SKU
  • Size, color and finish priorities
  • Application and destination market

  • Mix ratios and packing requirements
  • Required arrival window
  • Sample and document requirements
